01

What changed from ClickUp

Four things work differently. If you read nothing else, read these.

In ClickUpIn GoodDay
The brief was overwritten each time new client notes arrivedThe Brief never changes. Each round gets its own numbered ticket with that round's notes and files
Several date fields, and people worked to whichever one they openedOne box moves. Deadline is this round's date, and you change it every round
An SPV approval could carry a job further than intendedTwo separate approval boxes. An SPV approval cannot skip Julius
You told the editor in chat that it was coming backThe job returns on its own with a ticket, and the editor is told
02

Opening a job

One form. One job per deliverable. Never two deliverables on one ticket.

1

Click Production in the sidebar

2

Click the New Job tab

3

Name the job and pick the client

If the client is not in the list

They have not been set up yet. Open CRM, then Accounts, then New Account. Add them once and they are there for everyone. Do not type the client name into the job title instead. That breaks every report that counts work by client.

4

Set the Project Owner. This one matters most

Project Owner is not decoration

Whoever you name here is the person the job goes to at internal review. It puts the job in their list and sends them the message. Name the wrong person and the cut sits in the wrong list. Leave it empty and nobody is told at all.

5

Fill in the three dates

6

Paste the brief, add the asset link, set the size

On the job itself the estimate reads back in hours, because that is how GoodDay stores it. One day is 8 hours. Same number, shown differently. It only has to be roughly right, so the Workload view can show who is full.

7

Leave Priority on Normal unless there is a reason

High means this job jumps the queue ahead of the editor's other work. If everything is marked High, the flag stops meaning anything.

03

The three dates

This is where most of the confusion comes from, so here it is in one table.

BoxMeansWho moves itMoves?
DeadlineWhen this round is due back. At intake that is the first cut with the SPV.SPV or JuliusEvery round
Start / EndStart is when the editor picks it up. End is the date we promised the client.Producer at intakeOnly if we re-promise
Original client deadlineThe first date we ever promised, kept so a re-promise shows up.Nobody, after intakeNever
The rule, in one line

The editor works to Deadline and nothing else. So every time you send a job back, set the new Deadline in the same breath. If you do not, the editor is working to last round's date.

The reason it is that box and not a custom field: Deadline is the one date GoodDay shows at the top of every job, in every list, on the calendar, and in each person's My Work. It turns red by itself when it passes. It is also the only date that can fire the 7am due tomorrow message.

04

SPV at internal review

Your gate. Every cut passes through it, including every client round.

1

You get a message when a cut is waiting

2

Open the job and find Review result

Do not change the status by hand. Setting this field moves the job for you.

3

Pick Approved or Needs revision

Approved

Goes to FINAL REVIEW and lands in Julius's list. Your part is done.

Needs revision

Goes to INTERNAL REVISION. A numbered ticket is made and given to the editor, and they are told.

4

If it is going back, set the new Deadline

Ask the editor how long the round will take rather than guessing, then put that date in.

5

Write your notes on the new ticket

Never edit the Brief

The Brief is the original ask and it stays untouched for the life of the job. Overwriting it is the ClickUp habit that lost the studio its history. By round four nobody could see what was first agreed. Round notes go on the round's ticket.

05

Julius at final review

A different box from the SPV's. That is on purpose.

1

You get a message when one reaches you

2

Use Final approval, not Review result

Approved

Goes straight to CLIENT REVIEW, and today is stamped as the day the client got it.

Needs revision

Back to INTERNAL REVISION with a numbered ticket for the editor.

Approving sends it

The moment you approve, the board stamps today as the day the file went to the client. If it is actually going out tomorrow, that number is wrong and so is every report built on it. Only approve when the file is going out that day.

Why two boxes

If both gates read the same field, one SPV approval can carry a job through your gate and out the door. That happened in testing on 12 September. Separating them means your check cannot be skipped, including by accident.

2

07

The revision log

What replaced the ClickUp log.

Each ticket holds that round's notes and that round's files. Nothing is overwritten. Open a job a month later and you can read the whole history in order: the Brief at the top, then every round underneath it.

Lives on the jobLives on the round's ticket
The Brief, never editedWhat to change this round
Deadline, the live dateThe files and links for this round
The round countersWhether the round is finished
Why the date is not on the ticket

If it were, an editor with three open tickets would have three dates to compare. Keeping it on the job means one live date and one place to change it.

08

Attendance

Julius answers every request. The approval box does the rest.

1

Open Attendance and click Awaiting approval

2

Set the approval field. The status follows

What you setStatus becomesWhy it matters
Approve a sick daySICKKeeps illness separate from leave in the record
Approve a day offDAY OFFUnpaid unless you mark it paid
Mark as paid leavePAID LEAVEPayroll depends on this one. It is the only place paid leave is separated from an ordinary day off
DeclineDECLINEDCovers both sick and day off
Self-approval is watched, not blocked

GoodDay cannot stop a person approving their own day off. Instead, any change to an approval field raises an alert. It is not an accusation. It is what lets us say the record is trustworthy without locking anything down.

3

Use the standup to decide who gets the next job

It only works if people answer honestly, which means Standby has to be safe to say. If someone is punished for saying Standby once, the whole field stops being useful.

09

What runs itself

So you know what not to do by hand, and what the system will not do for you.

WhenWhat happens by itself
A job is created on the formThe SPV and Joshua are added to it, the editor is briefed, and a freelancer job goes to Julius
Status reaches INTERNAL REVIEWThe named SPV is messaged and the job enters their list
SPV approvesStatus becomes FINAL REVIEW, Julius is messaged, Review result is cleared
Julius approves at finalStatus becomes CLIENT REVIEW and the sent to client date is stamped
Anyone asks for changesStatus moves back, the round counter goes up by one, a numbered ticket is made and given to the editor, and the editor is told
Client approvesStatus becomes APPROVED, date done is stamped, the list is cleared
7am the day before a deadlineWhoever holds the job is reminded, but only while it is on their desk

What it will not do

  • Set the deadline for you. GoodDay cannot work out how long a round should take. Every new date is your decision.
  • Write the notes for you. The ticket is made empty. If nobody writes what to change, the editor gets a numbered ticket that says nothing.
  • Show a countdown in a message. GoodDay notifications cannot print a date or "5 days remaining". That is what the 7am message and the red overdue marker are for.
  • Undo a wrong approval. If you approve at final review, the file is marked as sent to the client today. There is no way to un-stamp that date.

The short version

  1. SPV: Review result at internal review. Set the new Deadline whenever you send it back.
  2. Julius at final review: Final approval. Approving means it goes out today.
  3. Julius at client review: Review result again. Client notes and files go on the ticket, never in the Brief.
  4. Everyone: one date box moves, and it is Deadline.
  5. Attendance: set the approval field, never the status. Paid leave is a deliberate choice.
