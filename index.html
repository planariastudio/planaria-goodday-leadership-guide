01

What changed from ClickUp

Four things work differently. If you read nothing else, read these.

In ClickUpIn GoodDay
The brief was overwritten each time new client notes arrivedThe original brief never changes. Each round's notes live on that round's numbered ticket and are never overwritten
Several date fields, and people worked to whichever one they openedOne box moves. Deadline is this round's date, and you change it every round
An SPV approval could carry a job further than intendedTwo separate approval boxes. An SPV approval cannot skip Julius
You told the editor in chat that it was coming backThe job returns on its own with a ticket, and the editor is told
02

Opening a job

One form. One job per deliverable. Never two deliverables on one ticket.

1

Open Quick add and choose Request

The + button at the bottom of the icon rail, on the very edge of the window. It works from anywhere in GoodDay, so you do not have to be on the Projects board first.

2

Pick Project Form

Why this route and not the tab on the board

Projects has a tab called Project Form as well, and for an ordinary member it opens the same form. For anyone with full access, which means Rashy and Julius, that tab opens the form designer instead. It looks almost identical, with the same fields in the same order, but you are editing the form itself rather than filling one in. Nothing you type there opens a job.

You can tell them apart in one glance. The designer has a grey strip above the form reading Form Designer, Settings, Submissions. The real form does not, and its heading bar reads Request › Project Form. Quick add always gives you the real one, whoever you are.

If you land in the designer, leave it alone

Do not edit fields to make a job work. Changing anything there changes the form for all thirteen editors and for every job opened after it. Close it and come back through Quick add.

Always use this form. If you open a bare task instead, half the automations will not fire.

3

Name the job and pick the client

If the client is not in the list

They have not been set up yet. Add them once in the CRM and they are there for everyone, on every job, for good. Do not type the client name into the job title instead. That breaks every report that counts work by client.

The CRM is not inside Staff Hub. It is its own section, reached from the icon rail down the far left of the window, third icon from the top.

That opens CRM Accounts. Every client the studio works with is one row. New Account is top right.

Name it once, name it right

Whatever you type becomes the name on every job and every report from then on. Renaming later is one edit in the CRM, but two accounts for the same client is a mess that has to be untangled by hand, so check it is not already there under a slightly different spelling before you add it.

4

Set the Project Owner

Project Owner is not decoration

Whoever you name here is the person the job goes to at SENT TO SPV. It puts the job in their list and sends them the message. Name the wrong person and the cut sits in the wrong list. Leave it empty and nobody is told at all.

Two editors on one job

GoodDay tracks one assignee per job and there is no way around that. So the lead editor goes in Assigned to, and anyone else cutting the same job goes in Other editors on this job. Naming somebody there puts the job on their board and sends them everything that happens to it, within seconds. It does not make them the assignee, so the job still belongs to one person and nobody is counted twice in workload.

Fill it at the same time you fill Assigned to. If you add a second editor later that works too, but they see nothing until you do.

5

Fill in the three dates

6

Paste the brief, add the asset link, set the size

On the job itself the estimate reads back in hours, because that is how GoodDay stores it. One day is 8 hours. Same number, shown differently. It only has to be roughly right, so the Workload view can show who is full.

7

Leave Priority on Normal unless there is a reason

High means this job jumps the queue ahead of the editor's other work. If everything is marked High, the flag stops meaning anything.

03

The three dates

This is where most of the confusion comes from, so here it is in one table.

BoxMeansWho moves itMoves?
DeadlineWhen this round is due back. At intake that is the first cut with the SPV.SPV or JuliusEvery round
Start / EndStart is when the editor picks it up. End is the date we promised the client.Producer at intakeOnly if we re-promise
Original client deadlineThe first date we ever promised, kept so a re-promise shows up.Nobody, after intakeNever
The rule, in one line

The editor works to Deadline and nothing else. So every time you send a job back, set the new Deadline in the same breath. If you do not, the editor is working to last round's date.

The reason it is that box and not a custom field: Deadline is the one date GoodDay shows at the top of every job, in every list, on the calendar, and in each person's My Work. It turns red by itself when it passes. It is also the only date that can fire the 7am due tomorrow message.

04

SPV review

Your gate. Every cut passes through it, including every client round.

1

You get a message when a cut is waiting

2

Open the job and find Review result

Do not change the status by hand. Setting this field moves the job for you.

3

Pick Approved or Needs revision

Approved

Goes to FINAL REVIEW and lands in Julius's list. Your part is done.

Needs revision

Goes to INTERNAL REVISION. A numbered ticket is made and given to the editor, and they are told.

4

If it is going back, set the new Deadline

You set this date, and you set it around the editor. Ask how long the round actually needs and use that answer, because a date built on what you wish were true is a date that slips.

The one rule: it has to land before the Original client deadline. If the editor's honest estimate pushes past it, that is a conversation to have today, with Julius and with the client, rather than on the day it is due.

Nothing moves this date for you. The moment a job goes into either revision state you and Julius both get a message asking you to set it, because a stale deadline is worse than no deadline.

5

Write what to change, on the ticket

The ticket for this round appears by itself, underneath the job. Open it and write the notes in Revision brief on the ticket. That is the box the editor reads, and it is the only place the notes need to go.

Each round gets its own numbered ticket, so nothing is ever overwritten.

This used to be written twice, once on the job and once on the ticket, because the box on the job was overwritten every round. That box is gone. The notes are written once, on the round they belong to, and they stay there.

Never edit the brief at the top

The brief at the top of the job is the original ask and it stays untouched for the life of the job. Round notes belong on the round's ticket. Overwriting the original is the ClickUp habit that lost the studio its history: by round four nobody could see what was first agreed.

6

The job comes back to you on its own

The editor does the work and sets the ticket to ROUND DONE. You do not chase anyone for a status change, and the editor never touches the job status.

If a job is sitting in a revision state with every ticket already closed, something has gone wrong. Tell Joshua.

05

Julius at final review

A different box from the SPV's. That is on purpose.

1

You get a message when one reaches you

2

Use Final approval, not Review result

Approved

Goes straight to SENT TO CLIENT, and today is stamped as the day the client got it.

Needs revision

Back to INTERNAL REVISION with a numbered ticket for the editor.

Approving sends it

The moment you approve, the board stamps today as the day the file went to the client. If it is actually going out tomorrow, that number is wrong and so is every report built on it. Only approve when the file is going out that day.

Why two boxes

If both gates read the same field, one SPV approval can carry a job through your gate and out the door. That happened in testing on 12 September. Separating them means your check cannot be skipped, including by accident.

06

Julius, once it is with the client

Now you are passing on what the client said, and the field changes back.

1

Use Review result again. It arrives empty

Approved

APPROVED. The date done is stamped and the job clears out of everyone's list.

Needs revision

CLIENT REVISION, with a numbered client ticket made for the editor.

2

Put everything the client sent onto the ticket

The ticket makes itself the moment the job goes into CLIENT REVISION. Open the job and it is waiting in the subtask list, empty and numbered for you.

Inside it there are two boxes. Both are yours.

Not on this ticket

There is no date on the ticket, and that is deliberate. The editor works to the Deadline at the top of the job, and setting it is the next step.

3

Then set the new Deadline on the job

07

The revision log

What replaced the ClickUp log.

Each ticket holds that round's notes and that round's files. Nothing on a ticket is overwritten. Open a job a month later and you can read the whole history in order: the original brief at the top of the job, then every round underneath it.

There is no longer a copy on the job. Revision brief and Files for this round exist once per ticket, so the question “what did round two actually ask for” always has an answer.

Lives on the jobLives on the round's ticket
The brief at the top, never editedWhat to change this round, kept for good
The original brief and the main assetRevision brief, this round's notes
Deadline, the live dateThe files and links for this round
The round countersWhether the round is finished
Why the date is not on the ticket

If it were, an editor with three open tickets would have three dates to compare. Keeping it on the job means one live date and one place to change it.

08

Who gets the next job

1

Read the two views before you assign

Nobody is asked how busy they feel any more. Two views answer it from work that is already on the board.

On whose desk, a tab on Projects, groups every job by Action required. That is the honest picture of right now, because a job sitting at SENT TO SPV or SENT TO CLIENT is open but is not on the editor. An editor with no rows under their name has nothing waiting on them.

Workload, also on Projects, is the forward view. It spreads each job's Estimate across its Start / End and stacks it per person, so you can see who is booked solid next week.

Workload shows what someone is committed to, not what they are doing

It is built from estimates and dates, not from hours anyone logged. If an editor finishes a job two days early, Workload still shows those two days as full. So read Workload for the weeks ahead and On whose desk for today. Where they disagree, On whose desk is right.

Both need three things on a job to work: an assignee, a Start and End, and an Estimate. The intake form makes all three compulsory, so the only jobs that fall through are freelancer ones with nobody assigned, and those show under Not assigned.

09

What runs itself

So you know what not to do by hand, and what the system will not do for you.

WhenWhat happens by itself
A job is created on the formThe SPV and Joshua are added to it, the editor is briefed, and a freelancer job goes to Julius
Status reaches SENT TO SPVThe named SPV is messaged and the job enters their list
SPV approvesStatus becomes FINAL REVIEW, Julius is messaged, Review result is cleared
Julius approves at finalStatus becomes SENT TO CLIENT and the sent to client date is stamped
Anyone asks for changesStatus moves back, the round counter goes up by one, a numbered ticket is made and given to the editor, the editor is told, and you and Julius are asked to set the new Deadline
Every ticket on a job is set to ROUND DONEThe job moves itself back to SENT TO SPV and the SPV is messaged
Client approvesStatus becomes APPROVED, date done is stamped, the list is cleared
7am the day before a deadlineWhoever holds the job is reminded, but only while it is on their desk

What it will not do

  • Set the deadline for you. GoodDay cannot work out how long a round should take, and we decided on purpose not to let it guess. Every new date is your decision. All the system does is ask you for it.
  • Write the notes for you. The ticket is made empty. If nobody writes what to change, the editor gets a numbered ticket that says nothing.
  • Show a countdown in a message. GoodDay notifications cannot print a date or "5 days remaining". That is what the 7am message and the red overdue marker are for.
  • Undo a wrong approval. If you approve at final review, the file is marked as sent to the client today. There is no way to un-stamp that date.

The short version

  1. SPV: Review result at SENT TO SPV. Set the new Deadline on the job whenever you send it back, and write Revision brief on the new ticket.
  2. Julius at final review: Final approval. Approving means it goes out today.
  3. Julius at SENT TO CLIENT: Review result again. Client notes go on the new ticket, in Revision brief, with anything they sent in Files for this round. Never in the brief at the top.
  4. Everyone: one date box moves, and it is Deadline.
  5. Attendance: set the approval field, never the status. Paid leave is a deliberate choice.
